# Sedatives: Side Effects, Dependence, and Addiction

What Are Sedatives?

Sedatives are prescription medications that slow activity in the brain and nervous system. They include benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and sleep aids, and they are sometimes called tranquilizers or depressants. Sedatives help treat anxiety, insomnia, and seizures.

Are There Any Risks Related to Sedatives?

In addition to the potential side effects, there are other risks related to sedative use. Physical withdrawal can develop within weeks or months.

However, anyone who is reducing or stopping sedative use should do so under medical supervision, as withdrawal effects can be severe and sometimes life-threatening. Additionally, while some of these medications aren’t meant for long-term use, they shouldn’t be stopped abruptly. Speak to your healthcare provider about how to taper safely.

Benzodiazepines

Stopping fast-acting benzodiazepines can lead to withdrawal symptoms within one to two days of the last dose. For long-acting benzodiazepines, it may take up to a week for symptoms to appear. Withdrawal may be similar to alcohol withdrawal.

A person may experience the following:

Rapid, shallow breathing

Tremors or trembling

Twitches and muscle spasms

Seizures

A rapid heart rate

Withdrawal from barbiturates can be challenging, and people may experience delirium tremens, a severe form of alcohol withdrawal that can be life-threatening.

Stopping short-acting barbiturates without medical support can lead to these effects:

Restlessness, tremulousness, and weakness within 12 to 20 hours

More severe tremulousness and weakness plus involuntary muscle contractions in 24 to 48 hours

Seizures, which can become severe and possibly lead to death, after 24 to 72 hours

Delirium, insomnia, confusion, hallucinations, overheating, and dehydration from days two to five

In Newborns

Taking certain barbiturates or benzodiazepines during pregnancy can impact the fetus. The newborn may show signs of malformation or withdrawal, including breathing problems, muscle weakness, and sensitivity to cold.

Using sleep aids alongside other depressants, sedatives, or opioids can increase the risk of serious side effects. It’s essential to follow your healthcare provider’s recommendations when taking these drugs.

Interactions, Contraindications, and More

It can be dangerous to use sedatives with other substances. Using them alongside other depressants can lead to a lowered heart rate and blood pressure, with potentially life-threatening consequences.

Contraindications

A provider may not prescribe sedatives if a person has any of these characteristics:

A history of dependence on alcohol or depressant drugs

Balance problems or an existing risk of falling

Breathing problems such as asthma

Pregnant or breastfeeding

Taking another drug that may interact with the sedative

A history of allergic reactions to sedatives

An older person or a child

Kidney or liver problems

Let your healthcare provider know about any drugs you are already taking and any existing health conditions, including the use of alcohol or recreational drugs.

Sedatives as Drugs of Abuse

While sedatives are prescribed to help people with certain conditions, they are also sometimes used illegally.

For example, flunitrazepam (Rohypnol) has been used to sedate victims of sexual assault; it also impairs the memory of the person targeted so they might not remember the assault.

[11]

What to Do in an Emergency

If someone takes too much of a sedative, mixes it with other sedatives, or shows signs of a severe reaction such as lowered breathing, decreased heart rate, or loss of consciousness, call 911.

[12]

The Takeaway

Sedatives are medications that can help manage anxiety, insomnia, seizure disorders, and other health issues.

Also known as depressants, sedatives include benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and sleep aids.

When misused or used with other depressants, sedatives can be life-threatening.

Always speak with your healthcare provider about the dose and side effects of a sedative drug prescribed to you to ensure that you are using it correctly.

Follow your healthcare provider's guidance when stopping a sedative to avoid withdrawal effects.
