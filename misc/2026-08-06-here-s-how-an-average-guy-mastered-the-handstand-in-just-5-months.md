The handstand is a great way to improve shoulder stability and balance, and so last year, YouTube's Geek Climber set himself the task of mastering this bodyweight move, setting himself the specific goal of improving his handstand until he is able to hold himself in position for 10 whole seconds.

Almost immediately, he realizes it's going to be much tougher than he imagined. Throughout the first two weeks of the challenge, he tries several different methods and approaches, including kicking back up into the handstand against a wall, walking up into a chest to wall handstand, the frogstand, but he had no success with any of them.

After mildly injuring his shoulders on Day 11, he takes some time to rest, and comes to the conclusion that the reason he is struggling so much with this particular move is that he has insufficient strength; he's only able to bench press around half of his own bodyweight. "My push strength was absolutely terrible," he says. "My wrists, elbows and shoulders were just too weak to stabilize myself."

In order to correct this, he sets himself a couple of intermediate goals during the ongoing challenge, starting with mastering the headstand, which eliminates the pressure on the wrists and elbows and focuses on core strength and balance. "Even though I struggled at first, eventually I was able to find the balance point, and successfully mastered the headstand pretty quickly," he says.

The second intermediate goal was to be able to comfortably hold an elevated pike press for 20 seconds. "This was the safest position where I felt comfortable pushing 100 percent to strengthen my weak wrists, elbows and shoulders," he says. "When I did back to wall handstands, I was unable to open my shoulders, so I could never balance myself."

105 days into the challenge, he is able to hold the pike press with open shoulders, and recommences his attempts at back to wall handstand kick-ups. "I immediately noticed a huge difference," he says. "I was able to practice for 30 minutes straight without my wrists or shoulders feeling sore, so I could finally focus on learning the technique."

On Day 122, he manages to hold a handstand without the support of the wall. "However, it was still very difficult for me to kick up directly into the right position without the wall; so many things had to go right at the same time." At this point, he shifts his focus to the tuck-up method. "I continued to practice, and practice, and practice. Multiple weeks flew by and I still couldn't seem to make progress."

It isn't until Day 154, when he receives the advice that he needs an experienced spotter to help him, that he begins to see some changes, and on Day 156, he finally holds the handstand for 11 seconds. "It felt amazing to learn another calisthenics move," he says.
