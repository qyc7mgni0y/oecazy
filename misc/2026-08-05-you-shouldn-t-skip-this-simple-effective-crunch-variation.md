# You Shouldn't Skip This Simple, Effective Crunch Variation

For many, the crunch is viewed as an unavoidable staple of core training. The exercise has long stood as a gold standard for every beginner trying to get ripped and strong abs.

But as our knowledge about core training and the body continues to grow, many fitness pros have stepped away from the crunch, and no longer view it as a super-effective, essential core exercise. Does this distancing mean that crunching is completely useless? Not necessarily—and this is where crunch variations come into play.

The vertical leg crunch is one of the few variations that still hold its weight as an effective core exercise. Let's break down how to do the exercise, the benefits you'll get from doing it, the muscles you'll work, and common mistakes some people make while doing the vertical leg crunch that you should avoid.

How To Perform the Vertical Leg Crunch

There are four key points to cover for efficiently and effectively performing the vertical leg crunch.

Lie flat on your back and bring your legs up into an “L” position. If there’s some bend in the knees, that’s okay.

Ground the lower back into the floor by flexing the abs, then bring the arms directly upwards as if you’re doing a Frankenstein walk.

While keeping the lower back grounded, reach upwards towards the toes by flexing the core and lifting the upper body off the ground.

[This Simple Effective](https://pipeprospect.com/783f80i23j/)

Reach until you physically can’t anymore, then slowly lower yourself back to the ground maintaining your torso positioning.

Your breathing should follow a consistent flow with the exhale occurring during the reach (concentric) and the inhale occurring during the lowering (eccentric) movement pattern.

The main muscles worked with the vertical leg crunch include the rectus abdominis and external obliques. You’ll also passively work the hip flexors, internal obliques, and deep core flexors, like the transverse abdominis, when exhaling during the movement.

Benefits of the Vertical Leg Crunch

There are a few key vertical leg crunch benefits worth noting when implementing it into your workouts.

1. Beginner Friendly

This exercise is great for beginners because it can help newer lifters learn how to contract their core without biasing their movement patterns and only doing cervical flexion—an issue that comes along with the traditional crunch.

By raising the arms, this exercise can teach beginners how to generate a contraction with the core in an easy self-cueing, digestible manner. The vertical leg crunch can also be an awesome progression for helping lifters conceptualize what it means to engage the core and ground the lower back—a skill needed for other movements like dead bugs and, eventually, hollow holds.

Common Vertical Leg Crunch Mistakes

While the movement is relatively easy to perform, over-eager exercisers can still make mistakes from time to time.

1. Un-Grounding the Lower Back

If the lower back is coming off the ground when you reach for your toes, that’s a pretty good indicator that you’re losing core tension throughout your reps. To cue this, place a folded towel under the back and try to press into it the entire set. This external cue can be useful for conceptualizing maintenance of tension.

2. Doing Lazy Reps

A large reason crunches get a bad rep is because they’re an easy exercise to perform without focus. They already have a short range of motion, so when we get lazy and shorten even more, we lose nearly all of the benefits of this movement. Just remember the old adage: speed hides deficiency. Move slowly and under control, instead of jerking through reps without any care.

To cue for better control, think about contracting the lower, middle, then upper abs in a ripple-like effect when you reach. Sometimes this mental cue can even help you achieve a slightly larger range of motion.
